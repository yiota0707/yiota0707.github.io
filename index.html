Research

Research Interests

I am interested in the design and analysis of intelligent systems that learn from experience, interact with other agents and operate in complex environments.

The questions below describe the main themes that currently shape my academic interests.

01

Multi-Agent Systems and Cooperative AI

How can autonomous agents learn to cooperate when their individual goals may conflict?

Multi-agent systems involve several intelligent agents learning, acting and adapting within a shared environment. I am interested in the mechanisms that influence cooperation, strategic behaviour and coordination between agents.

This includes reinforcement learning, partner selection, social dilemmas, adaptive interaction and the emergence of collective behaviour from individual decision-making.

Reinforcement Learning Multi-Agent Systems Cooperation Agent-Based Modelling

02

Intelligent Decision-Making

How should an intelligent system make decisions when information is incomplete, uncertain or constantly changing?

Many real-world systems cannot evaluate every possible outcome before acting. Intelligent agents must instead learn from experience, estimate future consequences and adapt their behaviour over time.

I am interested in learning algorithms that support autonomous decision-making under uncertainty, including reinforcement learning and simulation-based approaches.

Decision-Making Learning Algorithms Simulation Autonomous Systems

03

Artificial Intelligence for Engineering Systems

How can machine learning help us understand and monitor complex physical and engineering systems?

Engineering data is often noisy, incomplete and generated by systems whose behaviour changes over time. Machine learning can help identify patterns, detect unusual behaviour and support more informed technical decisions.

I am interested in intelligent diagnostics, condition monitoring, predictive methods and the application of AI to complex real-world systems.

Applied Machine Learning Diagnostics Condition Monitoring Engineering Data

04

Formal Methods and Mathematical Foundations

How can mathematical and formal tools help us reason more rigorously about computational systems?

Formal methods provide tools for expressing definitions, algorithms and mathematical arguments in a precise and verifiable way.

My interests include type theory, proof assistants, formal verification and the mathematical foundations of computer science and artificial intelligence.

Formal Methods Type Theory Agda Mathematical Logic

Research Collaboration

AIS-Based Vessel Movement Detection

Cyprus University of Technology

I worked in the Department of Electrical Engineering, Computer Engineering and Informatics on the implementation of algorithms for detecting vessel movements in maritime areas of interest.

The project used Automatic Identification System data and considered practical challenges including noise, reporting errors, missing information and message constraints. The work involved algorithm development, testing and analysis of vessel behaviour.

Python · Algorithm Development · Data Analysis · Maritime AIS Data

BSc Dissertation

2024–2025

A Study of Synthetic Differential Geometry in Type Theory

University of Nottingham

This project investigated the formalisation of Synthetic Differential Geometry using Homotopy Type Theory and the Agda proof assistant.

The work included formal definitions and verified proofs involving curvature, metrics, infinitesimal transformations and geometric structures. It also explored visual representations of spherical, planar and hyperbolic spaces.

Agda · Homotopy Type Theory · Formal Proofs · Mathematical Logic · LaTeX

Artificial Intelligence Project

Multi-Agent Coordination in Space Exploration

I developed a three-dimensional rover simulation to investigate coordination between autonomous agents operating in a Mars-inspired environment.

The project compared PPO, MADDPG and evolutionary algorithms under terrain, navigation, energy and coordination constraints.
